Learnings

What we know so far

Principles distilled from the experiments. These are working hypotheses — informed by evidence, but subject to revision as we learn more.

Most important insight

Context is the prompt

The single biggest driver of AI output quality is the context you establish before asking your question. Background, role, audience, constraints, format, examples — all of it shapes what comes out. Generic inputs return generic outputs. This isn't a limitation of the technology; it's a reflection of how language works.

Five working principles

01

Context is everything

Role, audience, format, and examples are not optional extras — they're the inputs that determine output quality. Invest time in setup. The prompt is the brief.

02

Model choice should match the task

Different models have meaningfully different default behaviors. GPT-4o is more decisive and action-oriented. Claude tends toward nuance and questioning assumptions. Neither is universally better — match the model to the moment.

03

Treat prompts like hypotheses

The first prompt rarely produces the best result. Iteration is the discipline. Write it, evaluate the output critically, identify what's missing or off, refine. This is strategy work — it should feel familiar.

04

AI multiplies, it doesn't replace

The sharpest outputs come when a human brings domain knowledge and judgment, then uses AI to accelerate, stress-test, or expand what they already know. Used without that foundation, AI tends toward confident mediocrity.

05

Ask AI to critique its own output

One of the most underused moves: after getting an output, prompt the model to identify what it might have missed, what assumptions it made, and where its reasoning was weakest. It's surprisingly honest and often catches real gaps.

Patterns we keep seeing

→

Volume ≠ importance in synthesis

When using AI to synthesize workshop notes, Claude weights by volume — more notes per cluster = more coverage. But the most strategically significant themes are rarely the ones with the most sticky notes. Human recalibration is always required.

→

Role mismatch is costly late

If the role you set in a prompt doesn't match the actual context, the errors surface downstream — usually when you've already built on the output. Be precise about role, audience, and context from the start rather than trying to correct later.

→

Garbage-in compounds quickly

Input quality matters more with AI than with human researchers, because AI synthesizes without flagging ambiguity unless asked. One-word sticky notes, ambiguous phrasing, and missing context all degrade output — often invisibly until you look closely.

→

The self-critique prompt unlocks hidden value

Asking the model to identify what it might have missed, what assumptions it made, and where it was uncertain — every single time, across every experiment — produced something worth acting on. Make this a default step in any AI-assisted workflow.

Tools we're using

Claude (Anthropic)

Best for nuanced, ambiguous, or research-oriented tasks. Strong at identifying unstated assumptions. Tends toward longer, more qualified responses.

↗

ChatGPT / GPT-4o (OpenAI)

Best for structured, action-oriented outputs. Fast and well-organized. Tends toward decisive framing — can be overconfident on ambiguous questions.

↗

Notion AI

Useful for within-document tasks (summarize, expand, rewrite). Not suitable for complex reasoning, but convenient for quick drafting and cleanup inside existing docs.

↗

Perplexity

Best for research and desk review tasks where citations matter. Significantly reduces the risk of hallucination for factual questions because it surfaces sources inline.
