About

What this is
and why it exists

Field Notes is a shared learning space for our team to document AI experiments, compare notes, and build a working knowledge base — from real work, not demos.

Why we built this

AI tools are evolving faster than most reading lists. The gap between "I tried something interesting" and "the team knows about it" is where useful knowledge disappears. This site exists to close that gap.

It's not a showcase. It's a working document — experiments that worked, ones that didn't, and the honest notes in between. The goal is a shared understanding of what these tools actually do in the context of our work, not a general theory of AI.

What counts as an experiment

An experiment is any intentional test of an AI tool against a real (or realistic) work task. It should have:

  • A specific goal — what were you trying to do?
  • A defined approach — what did you actually try?
  • An honest assessment of results — what came back, and was it useful?
  • A takeaway — what does this mean for how we work?

It doesn't need to be long. The goal is clarity and usefulness to someone else reading it, not comprehensiveness for its own sake.

What we're not trying to do

This isn't a place for general AI commentary, best-of lists, or theoretical exploration. Those resources exist elsewhere and are better done by others. We're focused on the specific question: how do these tools actually perform in the context of strategy and consulting work?

We're also not trying to be right. The experiments are documented honestly — including the ones where AI underperformed, produced misleading results, or required so much rework that it wasn't worth the time. That signal is just as valuable.

To add a new experiment

  1. Duplicate experiments/experiment-001.html and rename it with the next number.
  2. Update the page title, meta description, header content, and all experiment-specific fields in the sidebar card.
  3. Fill in the five main sections: Context & Goal, What We Tried, Results, Complications & Caveats, and Takeaway.
  4. Add the experiment to the list in experiments/index.html — copy one of the existing <article class="experiment-row"> blocks and update it.
  5. Add a card to the home page if it's one of the three most recent experiments. Update the stats bar numbers on the home page.
  6. If it generates a new principle or reinforces an existing one, update the Learnings page.
Keep it honest

The value of this resource is that it reflects what actually happened — not a polished version. Include the complications and the results you didn't expect. That's where the real learning is.
